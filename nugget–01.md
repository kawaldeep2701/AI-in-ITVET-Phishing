# Identifying Phishing Emails
## A self-learning nugget for First-Year IT Apprentices

### Introduction
*Workplace Scenario:*
You are working at the IT helpdesk. A colleague from the sales team calls you. They received an email saying their "company password will expire in 24 hours" and asking them to click a link to keep their account. They are panicking and ask you if it is safe. Before they click, you must analyze the email to see if it is a phishing attack.

### Explanation
Phishing emails try to steal your data by looking like real company emails. To check an email, always look at the sender address, check for urgency (like "act now!"), and inspect links before clicking.

| Red Flag | What to look for | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sender Address | Misspelled domains or public emails (@gmail.com) | Do not reply |
| Links | Mismatched URLs (text says HR, link goes to a hacker site) | Do not click |
| Attachments | Unexpected .zip or .exe files | Do not download |

### Worked Example
You receive an email from "HR Department" saying you got a bonus. The text says: Click here to view your bonus: www.company-hr.com/bonus. 
If you *hover your mouse* over the link, a small box appears at the bottom of your screen showing the real destination. If the real link is http://192.168.1.55/malware.exe, it is a phishing attempt. You should report it to the IT security team immediately.

### Knowledge Check

*1. Single Choice:* What is the safest way to check a link in an email?

- [( )] Click it quickly and close the tab if it looks strange.
- [(X)] Hover over it with your mouse to see the real URL.
- [( )] Reply to the sender and ask if the link is safe.

*2. Single Choice:* Which sender address is most suspicious?

- [( )] it-support@yourcompany.com
- [(X)] it-support@yourcompany-login-update.net
- [( )] john.doe@yourcompany.com

*3. Multiple Choice:* Which of the following are common signs of a phishing email?

- [[X]] Extreme urgency ("Your account will be deleted in 1 hour!")
- [[ ]] Correct spelling and professional layout.
- [[X]] Asking for sensitive information like passwords.
- [[X]] Unknown sender address.

### Summary
* Always hover over links to check the real destination URL.
* Be suspicious of emails demanding urgent action or passwords.
* Report suspicious emails to your IT security team immediately.
